# Pre or post fader: which tap point to record

Every digital mixer lets you take a recording feed from a point in the channel's signal path: the tap point. Where you take it decides what ends up in the file. Pick the wrong one and you can record your EQ mistakes, or a silent track because a fader was down.

**The short answer.** For a multitrack you will mix later, record from the earliest tap: the raw input after the preamp, or just after gain and trim and before EQ. Record post fader only for mixes and buses, not for channel tracks.

This page is general recording practice, not a maker's claim. What each mixer calls its tap points, and where they sit, is in that mixer's guide in the [mixer recording guides](/guides/record).

## The tap points, earliest first

### 1. The raw input, before the digital trim

Common names: X32 "Local" or "AES50" blocks on the card, Allen & Heath "Post Preamp" and Tie Lines, Avantis Record Send, Mackie "Mic Pre", Yamaha TF "Pre DG", the Soundcraft Ui24R inputs.

- **Pros.** Raw and unprocessed. Trim, EQ, mute and fader moves don't touch it. Best for virtual soundcheck, since playback comes back in at the same point.
- **Cons.** The preamp gain is still in it: a gain change during the show changes the track level, and a clip at the preamp is permanent. It is before the high-pass filter (HPF), so rumble and handling noise stay in.
- **Use it** for a multitrack you will remix later, and for virtual soundcheck. This is the usual default.

### 2. After gain and trim, before EQ

Common names: X32 "Pre-EQ", Allen & Heath "Post HPF", Yamaha TF "Pre HPF", Soundcraft Signature MTK "post-gain, pre-EQ".

- **Pros.** Clean tracks, with the digital trim included. A post-HPF version also removes rumble.
- **Cons.** Trim moves change the recording. On a post-HPF tap, the HPF setting is in the file for good.
- **Use it** for a clean multitrack when the engineer sets level with the trim.

### 3. After EQ and dynamics, before the fader

Common names: X32 "Pre-Fader", Allen & Heath "Post Comp" or "Post Delay", Behringer WING "POST PROC", Mackie "Ch Direct Out", Zoom L-12 (after the compressor).

- **Pros.** Sounds like front of house, so the tracks are ready to use.
- **Cons.** EQ and compression are baked in and can't be undone in the mix. A bad compressor setting ruins the take.
- **Use it** for quick release-ready recordings and streaming feeds.

### 4. After the fader

Common names: X32 "Post-Fader", Behringer WING "POST FDR", Yamaha TF "Post ON", Allen & Heath through mix sends.

- **Pros.** Follows the show mix exactly.
- **Cons.** A fader pulled down or a muted channel gives a silent track. Every fader ride is in the recording.
- **Use it** for bus and mix recordings, not for channel tracks.

### 5. The main mix and buses

Main L/R, aux, groups and matrix outputs: every mixer's "Main LR" or "ST".

- **Pros.** A reference mix. A matrix or audience-mic mix is handy for video.
- **Cons.** It is the front-of-house balance for that room, so loud stage instruments are often too quiet in it.
- **Use it** alongside the channel tracks, as a reference.

## What even the raw tap can't avoid

- **Gain changes reach the recording.** The earliest digital tap is still after the analog preamp. The X32 manual warns that front-of-house gain changes "can be heard in the recording". Some mixers can lock who changes gain: PreSonus (Preamp Permissions) and the Mackie DL32R (Access Limiting).
- **Digital trim vs analog gain.** Where a mixer has both (Allen & Heath, Yamaha "DG" for digital gain, Behringer WING, Mackie), the earliest tap is before the digital trim. Set the analog gain for headroom, then ride level with the trim and the fader.
- **Where the HPF sits.** The Ui24R inputs, Mackie "Mic Pre", Allen & Heath "Post Preamp" and Yamaha TF "Pre DG" are all before the HPF. If you want the low cut in the file, pick a tap after it.
- **Phantom power** sits at the preamp and doesn't depend on the tap. Switching it on while recording makes a pop on that track.
- **Shared stage boxes.** When two consoles share a stage box, the record feed is after its preamp, so whoever controls the preamp gain changes your recorded level. Allen & Heath Avantis has Gain Tracking to compensate the channel trim, but its Record Send and Tie Lines are before the trim. We found no gain compensation for X32, M32 or WING shared stage boxes.
- **Some mixers have a fixed tap.** On the Soundcraft Ui24R, the Signature MTK, the Zoom LiveTrak and USB 1-32 of the Yamaha TF, you can't move the USB tap, except the per-channel point on the TF. For a processed or post-fader version on the Ui24R, Soundcraft says to use an AUX set to POST or POST PROC.
